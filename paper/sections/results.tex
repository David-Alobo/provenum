\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Corruption test}

Table~\ref{tab:corruption} gives the main result. Matching by value alone caught 10 of the 307
corrupted numbers (3.3\%). Its 24 tables give 56,166 candidate values once proportions are also
read as percentages and differences between cells of a row are added, so a corrupted number
almost always rounds to some unrelated value and passes. Matching by surrounding words caught 57
(18.6\%). It seldom tied a prose number to the cell its words pointed to (11 numbers, of which 8
corruptions were caught), and it flagged 26 correct numbers whose words pointed at the wrong
cell.

With Claude Sonnet proposing addresses in blind mode, 134 numbers were anchored and every one of

no address, so the test says nothing about them. Coverage, not accuracy, is the limit of this
configuration.

\subsection{Local models}

Blind mode was not reliable with the local models. Qwen2.5-14B gave the right address for 43
numbers and a wrong one for 205; Qwen2.5-32B for 68 and 139. A wrong address cannot migrate a
number unless its value happens to round to the text, but in an audit each one is reported as a
possible error that the author has to look at.

Shortlist mode removes most of the difficulty. Table~\ref{tab:shortlist} compares the cells chosen
by the Qwen models with Claude's. Qwen2.5-32B linked fewer numbers than 14B but agreed with Claude
more often, and where the two Qwen models chose the same cell they agreed with Claude on 109 of
110 numbers.

\subsection{Migrating the manuscript}

The section was migrated in two passes. The first, with Claude's blind-mode addresses, replaced
124 numbers. The second offered the 245 numbers still typed by hand to Claude in shortlist mode;
it chose a cell for 211 and ``none'' for 34, and \texttt{migrate} replaced 188 where the formatted
value reproduced the text. After both passes 312 of the 369 decimal numbers in the section come
from data. The text extracted from the compiled PDF was identical before and after each pass,
which confirms that every replacement reproduces what was written.

The audit also found 28 numbers that no generated table produces. These are values computed by
hand or in one-off scripts, and they are the numbers most at risk after a rerun; the fix is to
add them to a table, not to the text.

\subsection{Model benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:bench} gives the results on the 311 gold numbers; Supplementary Table~S5 adds
the share right, false anchors and time per number in both modes. These are not the numbers of
the first experiment: they are the migrated numbers written back out, scored against a known
cell rather than against Claude, so the Qwen2.5 figures differ from those above. In blind mode no local model
traced half of them. The best, Qwen3-32B, gave the right cell for 152 (48.9\%), and most of its
wrong cells were rejected by the value check; 11 passed because the wrong cell held the same
rounded value. The 7 and 8~billion parameter models were right for at most 28 numbers. Only
Qwen2.5-32B abstained often (73 numbers), and it was the only model that declined all four
mentions of settings that come from no table; Qwen3-14B proposed a cell for two of them and every
other model for three or four.

In shortlist mode every model was right for most numbers, from 214 (Llama3.1-8B) to 274
(Qwen3-32B, 88.1\%). The cost of a wrong choice is different here. A wrong shortlist choice
holds the same value as the right cell, so it migrates, renders correctly, and points the text at
the wrong cell for the next rerun. Of the cells each model chose, the share that were right
ranged from 68.8\% (Llama3.1-8B) to 94.2\% (Qwen2.5-32B, 242 of 257); Qwen3-32B chose more cells
and was right for 90.4\% of them (274 of 303). No local model reached the point where its
shortlist choices could be accepted without review. The four settings with no cell were
anchored in shortlist mode only by Qwen3-8B and Llama3.1-8B (three each).

Model size mattered more than model family: within each family the 32 billion parameter models
were best in both modes, and Llama3.3-70B, at twice that size, was below both 32B models. Time per
number ranged from 0.5 to 2.0~s, so one pass over the 311 numbers took at most about 10 minutes.

\label{sec:res-review}

Without a model, a single matching cell was weak evidence. Of the 78 numbers that exactly one cell
reproduced, 10 were linked where the hand migration had left the number typed. One, a standard
deviation, is a correct link. The other 9 (11.5\% of the 78) are coincidences: for example, ``a
spread of 8.9 points'' matched an interval bound, and ``odds ratio 10.5'' a share column.

With Qwen3-32B and the hand specification, 249 numbers were linked and 228 of them (91.6\%) to the
cell the migration uses. Of the 21 that differed, 20 were in the \emph{to confirm} list. The one in
\emph{linked} was a standard deviation the migration had left typed, and the model's cell for it
was correct. Read one by one, the 21 differences fall into three groups:
\begin{itemize}
\item 8 are wrong cells;
\item 10 are the same quantity stored in another table, such as Vina's baseline rate in both the
summary and the comparison table (8 clearly, 2 probably);
\item 3 link numbers the migration left typed, correctly or probably correctly.
\end{itemize}

Supplementary Tables~S7 and~S8 list every disagreement and every single match. On this
reading, 8 of 249 links (3.2\%) point to the wrong cell, and all 8 were in the list the author
is asked to confirm. None of them could have been caught by value, since every cell offered in
shortlist mode holds the written value. They are cells of another engine, arm or interval bound,
such as the Holm p-value of 0.37 that the 6~\AA{} and 8~\AA{} arms share.

The model's choices were reproducible. Three repeats of the run gave the same choice for all 307
numbers as the first run.

With the guessed specification Qwen3-32B linked 25 fewer numbers (26 lost, 1 gained). For 17
of the 26, the guessed specification formats the matching cells differently from the text, for
example to three decimals where the text has two, so the application left them typed. For the
other 9, the model chose none of the cells as the guessed specification names them. The 17
differences from the migration were all in the \emph{to confirm} list. Checking the guessed
formats is therefore worth the author's time before a run.
